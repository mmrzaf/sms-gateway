# 009. UUIDv7 Identifiers and Keyset Pagination

Status: Accepted

## Context

Identifiers are generated at high rates by several processes. Listings must paginate efficiently over large tables, time-range queries must be served by indexes, and tables will later be partitioned by time.

## Decision

All primary keys except `workers.id` are UUIDv7, generated by the application. Listings use keyset pagination over `id`. Time-range filters are translated into UUIDv7 bounds, so the customer-scoped primary-key order serves them. Partitioning, when introduced, is by range of `id`.

## Alternatives considered

**Random UUIDv4.** Rejected: random index insertion order, and IDs carry no time information.

**Database sequences.** Rejected: IDs are needed before insert in batch operations, sequences reveal volumes to customers, and they complicate sharding.

**Offset pagination.** Rejected: cost grows with the offset, and results shift when rows are inserted during paging.

## Consequences

Positive:

- Index-friendly insertion order; one customer-scoped index serves lookups, listings, time filters, and reports.
- Stable pagination whose cost does not depend on page number.
- Partitioning by `id` keeps `id` as the primary key.

Negative:

- The ID's embedded time is the application's time at generation, a few milliseconds from `accepted_at`; time filters are exact with respect to the ID.
- IDs reveal the approximate creation time of a resource.
